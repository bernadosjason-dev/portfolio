Product · founder build

Athlos: pickleball in the Philippines, in one app.

Players were finding games through “+1” lists in group chats, booking courts by text, paying by screenshot and tracking tournaments on paper. Athlos puts all of it in one place — ratings, open play, court booking, tournaments, chat and a portal for the venues — and automates the admin that used to eat an organizer’s day.

02

What it does

Players

An Athlos Rating from 2.000 to 8.000 for singles, doubles and mixed. Only refereed games move it, so the number means something. Form, clutch games and partner chemistry on top.

Open play

Organizers schedule a session in under a minute and share one short link. Players join, pay ahead by GCash with a receipt, and drop to an automatic waitlist when it is full. Every session gets its own group chat. Check in, join a fair queue, and get called to your court by push.

Courts

A court directory with maps and directions. Pick a free slot, the slot is held while you pay by GCash, the venue confirms the receipt. Can’t make it? Pass the booking to a friend. Refunds follow written rules, not arguments.

Tournaments

Set up in five steps. Groups + knockout, round robin or elimination. The schedule builds itself: every match gets a time, a court and a referee, and the knockout runs as the evening main event.

Referees

Certified referees with their own desk and ID. Assigned automatically, rebalanced when a match moves, called to court on their phone.

Venues

A separate portal to approve payments, add walk-ins and run open play, plus the venue’s own booking page with no Athlos branding and a short link for its Facebook Page. App bookings, walk-ins and open play share one calendar, so nothing double-books.

Messaging

Direct messages, club and venue chats and open play group chats, with emoji reactions, unsend, archive and delete, and push notifications.

03

What’s automated

  • Scheduling. Draw the bracket and every match is timed, placed on a court and given a referee. Move a match and the referee load rebalances.
  • Reminders and call-to-court. Scheduled jobs remind players before their match and buzz their phone when their court is ready.
  • Booking holds and refunds. Unpaid holds expire on their own; when a venue declines or cancels, the player chooses a refund or a free rebook and the deadline is tracked.
  • Show-up record. No-shows and late cancels are recorded from check-ins, so organizers can see who reliably turns up.
  • Fees by date. The booking fee switches from the pilot rate to the launch rate on a set date, with no code change; every booking keeps the fee it was made with.
  • Policy updates. Publishing a new Terms or Privacy version notifies every player and asks them to accept it on their next visit.
  • Ratings. Verified results update ratings and leaderboards with no one entering numbers by hand.
  • Monitoring. An agent checks errors, failed jobs and support messages every hour, and drafts a fix branch when it finds a real bug.
04

Launch content, rendered from code

The launch-week reels are not edited by hand. Each one is a web page animated frame by frame, captured in a headless browser and encoded to video. The sound effects and music are synthesized in Python, so there is nothing to license. Changing a price or a caption is one edit and a re-render, which is how the venue reel was updated the same evening the booking fee changed.

05

How it’s built

Next.js on Vercel, Supabase for Postgres, auth, storage, realtime and scheduled jobs. Business rules live in the database behind row-level security, so the phone app, the venue desk and the organizer console all enforce the same rules, and every rule change ships with a scenario test that runs against a real Postgres. Web push for alerts. Designed, built and run solo.
