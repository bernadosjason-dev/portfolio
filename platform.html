01

Why the seam matters

The whole idea

When you quote a fit-out job you count 18 carcasses, 42 m of edging, 36 hinges. Those numbers price the job. Then they get re-typed into a schedule, re-counted on site, and re-checked at turnover — by different people, from different files, at different times.

Every re-type is a place the job can quietly lose money. Datum takes the quantity off the drawing once. The same figure that priced the cabinet is the figure that schedules its fabrication, appears on the site log, and gets signed off at handover.

General project management tools cannot do this, because they never knew what a carcass was. They start at the schedule — after the number has already been typed twice.

03

Who this is for

Datum is deliberately narrow. It was built inside a modular manufacturing business, for the way that trade actually quotes and builds — which means it fits some contractors very well and others not at all.

Built for

  • Modular kitchen and cabinetry makers
  • Fit-out and joinery contractors
  • Office and commercial interior firms
  • Design-build outfits quoting from sketches and site measurements
  • Teams quoting often, in volume, against a rate card that moves

Not built for

  • Heavy civil and infrastructure
  • Vertical construction and structural works
  • Enterprise contractors already running Procore or Autodesk Build
  • Anyone who needs accounting, payroll or BIM — Datum does not replace those
